06 / KEY TAKEAWAYSCREDIT RISK / ANALYST NOTES

What the evidence tells us.

INSIGHT / 01

Class imbalance matters.

22.12% of clients in the active dataset have the next-month default flag. Any predictive model should be assessed beyond simple accuracy, using recall, precision and calibration.

INSIGHT / 02

History needs context.

Six months of billing and repayment observations are available. These are different cash-flow and statement concepts, and should not be conflated.

INSIGHT / 03

Do not overclaim.

The dataset lacks realised losses and contractual exposure at default. It does not support a defensible expected-loss or recovery-rate estimate by itself.

About the analysis.

Dataset & provenance

Default of Credit Card Clients, compiled by I-Cheng Yeh and colleagues. 30,000 Taiwanese credit-card clients; records from April–September 2005, with a next-month payment-default target. UCI dataset ID 350, DOI 10.24432/C55S3H ↗, CC BY 4.0. The dashboard's opening summary uses the dataset's documented counts and published descriptive statistics; it does not claim to have independently recomputed all 30,000 raw records.

Published averages and quartiles: Descriptive statistics, Table 3 ↗. Outcomes: UCI-derived outcome counts ↗.

Analytical guardrails

  • Observed default incidence ≠ prospective probability of default.
  • Credit limit ≠ drawn balance ≠ exposure at default.
  • Undocumented categories in repayment and education codes require caution.
  • Historical 2005 data are unsuitable for direct contemporary credit decisions.
  • Demographic variables require fairness review before any modelling or use in decisions.

The downloadable project includes the Python analysis pipeline, reproducible SQL queries and checks.
